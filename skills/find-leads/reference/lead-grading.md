# Grading a lead-scan hit: REAL or ROUTINE

`lead_scan` matches a keyword list against whole agenda documents, so most of what it returns is ordinary
business that happens to contain one of the words — server precision is roughly 30%. **Every hit gets
graded before anything reaches the rep.** Only a REAL hit is staged (F1), scored (F2), and
cross-referenced against QuickBase (F5). A ROUTINE hit is set aside silently; it appears only in the
closing count line.

## REAL

A specific project, procurement, grant, bond, CIP, or budget line that could buy what Bliss sells. That
covers playground, shade structure or canopy, splash pad, surfacing, site furnishings, pavilion,
bleachers, courts, fitness, and trail amenities, at planning, funding, design, or bid stage.

Examples (all public records):

- **Spalding County, GA** — a $148,140 sole-source purchase of the Tyus Park playground.
- **Bossier City, LA** — the Meadowview splash pad.
- **Alexandria, LA** — the Cheatham Park pavilion bid.

## ROUTINE

Minutes approvals, maintenance or repair of existing items, road resurfacing, judicial courts, legal
"exercise", generic park mentions, and staff or HR items.

Examples:

- A council item approving the minutes of the previous meeting, or accepting a routine staff report.
- A county awarding a road-resurfacing contract, or a line item repairing an existing roof, HVAC unit,
  or restroom fixture with no play or site-amenity scope.
- A municipal court annex addition (the judicial sense of "courts"), or a parks-department hire, pay-band,
  or policy item with no project attached.

## Judging the edge cases

- A keyword match alone is not enough. "Basketball courts" can be REAL; "court" in the judicial sense is
  ROUTINE. "Picnic table" can be REAL; "motion to table" is ROUTINE. "Park" as its own word can be a
  REAL context; "parking" is ROUTINE. "Shelter" for a pavilion or gazebo project can be REAL; "animal
  shelter" and "storm shelter" are ROUTINE.
- **Maintenance of an existing item is ROUTINE; a replacement or upgrade project with a scope and a
  budget line is REAL.** Repairing a cracked slide is ROUTINE; replacing the playground and its surfacing
  is REAL.
- A generic mention ("our parks are important to the community") with no project, money, or date is
  ROUTINE. A named park with a scope, a stage, and a date is REAL.
- Grading happens on what the source shows. If it's ambiguous whether a project is specific and
  purchasable, grade it ROUTINE and let the closing count line carry it — a wrong REAL lead that gets
  pitched costs more than a ROUTINE one that gets set aside.
